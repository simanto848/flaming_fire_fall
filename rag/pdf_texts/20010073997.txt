INTRODUCTION

two-phase non-premixed spreading flames, such as those that may occur due to accidents in
inhabited buildings and orbiting spacecraft. The role of buoyant convection on flame spread is
substantial, especially for thermally-thick fuels. With suitable assumptions, deRis [1] showed
that the spread rate (Sf) over thick fuels is given by:

CONCLUSIONS
Our results indicate that, in contrast conventional understanding, steady spread can occur
over thick fuels in quiescent microgravity environments, especially when a radiatively active
diluent gas such as CO2 is employed. We propose that this is due to radiative transfer from the

behavior with decreasing bed thickness is demonstrated. It was found that foam fuels enabled

experiments. These results axe being used to define a space flight experiment called Radiative
Enhancement Effects on Flame Spread (REEFS) planned for the International Space Station in the
Combustion Integrated Rack facility.
